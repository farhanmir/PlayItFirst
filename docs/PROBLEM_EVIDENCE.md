# Why rehearse a business before opening?

Play It First lets a prospective operator explore a commercial space, arrange a café, and run a shared service shift before committing to a layout or equipment plan. The evidence supports **testing decisions early**; it does not establish that this game predicts a real café's revenue or prevents business failure.

| Problem to explain | Primary evidence | What the game can honestly demonstrate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A founder must choose a location and estimate costs before opening. | The [U.S. Small Business Administration's planning guide](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) recommends market research and calculating startup costs before launch. Its [launch guide](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/launch-your-business/) says location affects regulations, target-market access and costs. | Bring a specific property's stated size and asking rent into an early scenario, then let the team discuss tradeoffs. Listing terms are inputs, not a validated profit forecast. |
| Layout can change how service feels and flows. | In a [peer-reviewed exploratory study of two Dutch office canteens](https://research.wur.nl/en/publications/the-influence-of-facility-layout-on-operations-explored/), researchers observed 47 customers and reported different congestion and waiting patterns across layouts. The sample was small and selected for convenience, so this is a reason to test layouts, not a universal café effect size. | Let players move the order counter and pickup shelf, walk the route and compare what happens in a simulated shift. Distances and wait times in the game are model outputs. |
| Opening a business is consequential, but survival statistics do not identify one cause. | [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics data](https://www.bls.gov/spotlight/2024/business-employment-dynamics-twentieth-anniversary/home.htm) report that 57.3% of establishments born in 2018 survived five years. This is an all-industry cohort measure; it cannot attribute closures to layout, rent or a lack of rehearsal. | Frame Play It First as an inexpensive way to expose questions and coordinate a team before opening, without claiming it increases survival. |

## Pitch wording

“Choosing a space and its costs is an early business decision, and the way people move through a service space can affect waiting and congestion. Play It First turns a property into a playable rehearsal so a team can find awkward routes and discuss assumptions before opening.”

For this demo, property geometry and listing terms have their own provenance; customer demand, operating costs and service outcomes are simulation assumptions. Compare alternatives inside the same scenario, and label any modeled improvement as modeled. A real-world validation study would need actual operators, measured service times and outcomes after launch.
